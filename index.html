The Airbox Flaws We're Solving

Every OEM airbox sacrifices volumetric efficiency for production budget, noise emissions, and tooling simplicity. Here is why your bike is breathing through a bottleneck.

PARALLEL-TWIN INTAKE CROSS-TALK

APRILIA RS 457

270° CRANK

The Stock Problem: The RS 457's 270° parallel twin fires cylinders at uneven 270° and 450° crankshaft intervals. The OEM airbox uses identical symmetrical rubber funnels placed directly adjacent to one another. During the fast 270° intake interval, Cylinder 1's intake stroke robs Cylinder 2 of charge density, creating acoustic cancellation and starving Cylinder 2 by up to 7.4% air mass at 9,500+ RPM.

The XCD Solution: Asymmetric staggered velocity stacks with independent conical throat tapers. By varying the bellmouth entry height and acoustic acoustic standoff between Cylinders 1 and 2, we decouple the resonance waves and equalize cylinder-to-cylinder fill rates.

RE CONTINENTAL GT 650

648cc TWIN

The Stock Problem: The 648cc twin has huge displacement demand but breathes through narrow, squarish OEM rubber intake tracts. At 4,200 to 6,000 RPM, severe intake pulse reversion pushes atomized fuel mist backward out of the throttle bodies into the airbox (fuel standoff mist), causing a noticeable hesitation and sluggish roll-on response.

The XCD Solution: Long-runner AeroTwin velocity horns with anti-reversion boundary rings. The extended acoustic runner captures the fuel standoff pulse and rams it back down the throat, converting wasted fuel mist into +3.2 Nm of immediate roll-on torque.

KTM 390 PLATFORM

GEN 2 & GEN 3

The Stock Problem: Big single-cylinder engines generate violent, pulsating intake vacuum spikes reaching down to -35 kPa. The thin factory plastic intake funnel flexes and flutters inward under this massive cyclic depression, disrupting the laminar intake boundary and causing top-end revs to hit a brick wall past 8,800 RPM.

The XCD Solution: FEA-reinforced ASA-Carbon composite walls with 6 solid outer perimeters and integrated radial stiffening ribs. Zero wall deflection under maximum vacuum draw, holding an unbroken 180° bellmouth geometry up to the 10,200 RPM redline.

YAMAHA R15 V3 / V4

155cc VVA

The Stock Problem: At 7,400 RPM, Yamaha's Variable Valve Actuation (VVA) switches to a high-lift intake cam lobe. However, the OEM intake funnel length is optimized exclusively for low-cam velocity. When the high cam engages, the air column stalls briefly, blunting what should be an aggressive power surge.

The XCD Solution: Stepped dual-taper venturi geometry designed to sustain high acoustic velocity through the exact 7,400 RPM transition point, allowing the high-lift cam to pull uninterrupted all the way to 12,000 RPM.
